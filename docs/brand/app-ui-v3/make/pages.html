HowThe site’s dominant colour is read from the published screenshot (the most common saturated colour, ignoring white, black and greys). If a site has none, it uses the Publish wash. A navy site comes out as a soft periwinkle, a yellow one as butter: never the raw brand colour, never grey mud.

Where each is used

Inside the app

A wash is a moment, not a background: an empty state, a card that introduces a step, the drop zone, the preview stage on a site page. Lists, tables, settings and the session player stay plain.

On the website and in videos

Each section sits on its step’s full wash (Publish, Test, Review), the hero on all three. The same tokens, so the app and the site match.

Site-tinted

After a site is published, its page uses --wash-site, made from the site’s dominant colour. Hue from the site; lightness and chroma fixed by Aero; a little Publish peach always mixed in.

Grain

A fine noise layer (--grain) sits on every full wash so wide gradients never band on 8-bit screens. Cards don’t need it.

WhyLight or dark is automatic: Aero reads the prototype’s background colour where the panel sits and picks the plate that reads best (a light plate on coral, a dark one on a dark app). Words sit on the frosted plate; glass is only on the small chrome: the grip, minimise, the recording sign and the minimised tile. “Done” is the ink button and “Couldn’t do it” sits beside it at the same size, so giving up never feels like failing. There is no timer for testers, only “Recording” with a dot at the top centre.
