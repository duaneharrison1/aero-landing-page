What a tester sees.

The person opening a test link has never heard of Aero and is doing Mia Chen a favour. Every screen tells them who asked, what happens, what is recorded and that they can stop. Example: Mia Chen at Kestrel Studio testing Pocket, a payments prototype at pocket.aeroapp.ai.

00

Five rules for every tester screen

Drawn from the bugs (B15, B16), the review’s tester section and Duane’s note that the panel has to work over prototypes of any colour.

01Say it before asking

Who asked, what happens, what is recorded, who sees it, stop any time. Before any browser prompt.

02One permission step

Mic and tab on one card, in order. Four modals today become two screens.

03Solid where you read

The task panel is a frosted plate (86% white), so it reads on any prototype. Glass only on small floating chrome.

04Never in the way

The panel moves to any corner and folds to a pill. On a phone it docks below the prototype instead of over it.

05Say what’s lost

Every failure says what still works and what doesn’t: no mic means no transcript; no tab means voice only.

Why the order is mic first, then tab

The voice carries the value: without it there is no transcript, summary or friction points. Asking for it first, while the tester is most willing, gets more audio. The tab share is the bigger ask and comes second, with the facts already on screen.

Browser notes

  • Chrome and Edge: the tab prompt is a single Allow (preferCurrentTab).
  • Firefox and Safari can’t share a single tab: they show a window or screen list. The helper line changes to “Choose the window with Pocket in it.”
  • Start enables as soon as the mic is decided (allowed, blocked and accepted, or skipped).

05

The task panel over the prototype

Prototypes can be any colour, and Aero can’t read their pixels (a Figma embed is a cross-origin frame), so the panel can’t adapt to what’s behind it. It has to work everywhere as it is. Today’s panel is smoky glass with white text: fine on dark prototypes, unreadable on white ones. The panel is also inside every recording, so it shows which task was on screen in the replay.

AA. PlateRecommended
  • The shared plate token (86% white, 22px blur), a dark hairline outside a white one, and a soft shadow. Measured 14:1 or better on all six test prototypes.
  • Glass only on the Done button: it picks up the prototype’s colour at its edge, so the panel feels part of the moment without risking the text.
  • Same component for Figma (in the page) and HTML (injected into the site, in a closed shadow root).
BB. V3 glass panelOption
  • The whole panel is V3 dark glass; the engine raises the tint until the text passes.
  • Prettier on calm prototypes, but over busy ones the tint ends up nearly opaque anyway, and the refracted edge shows in every recording.
  • Safari and Firefox get frost only. Keep for floating chrome, not for reading.
CC. Docked rail (Figma only)Option
  • Never covers the prototype; the whole list is visible.
  • The Figma frame shrinks to fit: fine for phone frames, 22% smaller for desktop frames.
  • Impossible for HTML tests (the site is its own tab), so it would be a second design to keep. Use the docked idea on phones only.

Where glass is used, and why

Duane: “the testing screen uses a glass effect but we don’t use that elsewhere.” With V3 now the app’s button glass, the tester screens use it the same way: on buttons and floating chrome, never on text you have to read.

WhereGlass?Why
Primary buttons on tester cards (Start, Start the test, Copy link)V3 primary, subtleThe same quiet glass as the app’s primary actions. On a white card it barely shows, which is right.
Done in the task panelV3 primary, subtleThe one place the prototype’s colour reaches the panel: a thin reflection on the button’s edge. The label stays ink-on-white AA because the engine nudges the fill.
Minimised pillV3 secondary, strength 1.6Small floating chrome over unknown colour: exactly what the research says glass is for. The engine raises opacity until the label passes: lowest measured 4.6:1, over black.
Task panel bodyNo. Near-opaque plate with a light blurContent needs guaranteed contrast. Glass over a busy prototype either fails or becomes opaque. The panel is in every recording, so refraction would also warp the prototype in the replay. Safari and Firefox would get only frost.
Welcome and thank-you cardsNoSolid paper on a dim or a soft Test wash. Reading, not floating.
Today’s smoky glass (white text)RemoveFails on four of six prototypes (measured 2.4:1 on white). It’s also the only glass panel in Aero, which is what Duane spotted.

Accessibility review

  • Contrast: the panel title and body pass 4.5:1 on all six prototypes (measured). Secondary text on the plate uses ink-2, not ink-3.
  • Focus: Start and each new task title receive focus; Escape folds the panel to the pill; the pill’s Show restores it.
  • Announcements: “Task 3 of 4: Freeze your card”, “Recording”, “Sent” are aria-live polite. Errors are role=alert.
  • Ratings: a radio group with text labels, keys 1 to 5. No emoji.
  • Colour is never the only signal: every state has words (Rec, Muted, Offline, Sent).
  • Motion: the pulse and level meter stop under prefers-reduced-motion.
  • Targets: 40px buttons on desktop, 46px on phones; the rating buttons are 48px tall.
