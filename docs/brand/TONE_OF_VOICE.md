# Aero tone of voice

Use this file every time you write words a designer, a tester or a website visitor will read: app labels, tester screens, emails, errors, empty states, the website, content pages, videos and social posts. Internal docs and code comments are exempt.

## In one line

A designer who has shipped a lot of prototypes and tested them with real people, explaining it to a colleague. Calm, plain, quick, a little warm. Never salesy, never "AI magic".

## Who we write for

- **People who made something and need it online:** a Claude artifact, a Lovable app, an HTML folder.
- **UI and product designers** who want feedback on a Figma prototype before a review.
- **UX researchers** who want cheap think-aloud sessions without a sales call.
- **PMs and founders** checking whether an AI-built idea makes sense to anyone.
- **Students** with portfolio and case-study work.
- **Testers:** friends, clients or classmates who opened a link and have never heard of Aero. Their screens need the plainest words of all.

International, English, often reading quickly between other work.

## The rules

1. **Plain words.** Publish, share, test, watch, review. Not deploy, leverage, unlock, empower, seamless, revolutionary, all-in-one, game-changing, magic.
2. **Short.** One idea per sentence; most sentences under 20 words.
3. **Specific and true.** "Live in seconds to a minute." "Transcript about a minute after they finish." Every number is true today, or labelled "Example".
4. **AI stays in the background.** Name it at two moments only: on the way in ("built with Claude or ChatGPT") and on the way out ("transcribed and summarised"). Never "AI-powered" and never the headline.
5. **No clichés.** Duane flagged lines like "Live in minutes, however you made it" as AI-sounding. Prefer a concrete line: "Paste a Figma link, drop an HTML folder, or ask Claude. It's live."
6. **Warm, not cute.** At most one light touch per screen. No exclamation marks, and never in errors.

## Punctuation and style
- No em dashes or en dashes in prose. Use a full stop, comma, colon or brackets. Ranges with "to".
- Sentence case everywhere: headings, buttons, menu items, emails.
- Buttons are plain verbs with no "…": "Publish", "Copy link", "Start test".
- British spelling (colour, organise, analyse).
- "I", not "we", only in a founder's own voice. There is no founder voice on the site; product copy uses neither.

## Product words

| Use | Avoid |
|---|---|
| prototype, site, build | product experience, digital asset |
| publish, put online, it's live | deploy (on the site), launch your vision |
| test, test link, session | study, research campaign |
| tester, people | respondents, participants, users (meaning testers) |
| watch, hear, review | capture insights, unlock learnings |
| friction point, in their own words | pain point analysis |
| transcript, summary, themes across sessions | insights platform, AI insights |
| Figma link, HTML folder, one prompt | seamless import, integrations |
| free to start, from $3 a month | affordable, cheap, plans for every team |

**The three steps are Publish, Test, Review** (agreed 23 Oct). Not "Watch" and "Read": by the time you look, the session is already transcribed and summarised. Review means: read the summary and friction points, dig into the detail, or compare across sessions.

## Lines Duane likes (reuse)
- "One prompt and it's live."
- "Made for people who make things."
- "In their own words."
- "From prompt to first session."
- "Publish your prototype. Watch people use it." (working headline)

## Truth in copy
- Don't say "live transcript" or show typing during a session: transcripts come about a minute after.
- Don't imply webcam or faces: Aero records the screen and the voice.
- Don't say testers' mouse movements are recorded; a browser-tab recording doesn't show them.
- Don't promise panels, recruiting, seats or compliance. "Bring your own testers."
- Plans and limits only from `PRICING-FACTS.md`.

## Tester screens
The person reading has never heard of Aero and is doing someone a favour. Tell them what will happen, what is recorded, how long it might take (not a fixed time: sessions have no set length), and that they can stop at any time. Thank them at the end.
